# Reviewer-Risk Rubric

## Serious

Likely to threaten validity, contribution, or publishability and usually requires author action before submission.

Examples:

- construct definition and measurement do not align;
- duplicated or incorrect measurement items;
- contradictory sample sizes or results;
- unsupported causal or mediation claim central to the paper;
- manipulation confound undermines construct interpretation;
- contribution duplicates established findings without a distinct theoretical prediction;
- missing study information prevents evaluation or replication.

## Moderate

Important but usually repairable through clarification, reanalysis, additional justification, or focused rewriting.

Examples:

- unclear reference category or coding;
- incomplete degrees of freedom or indirect-effect reporting;
- weak transition from results to contribution;
- boundary conditions not adequately delimited;
- practical implications broader than the tested context;
- citation-reference inconsistencies.

## Minor

Does not materially affect validity but reduces polish or compliance.

Examples:

- inconsistent capitalization or hyphenation;
- minor citation formatting differences;
- table-note omissions that are obvious from context;
- isolated grammatical or punctuation problems.

## Report Format

For each risk, state:

1. severity;
2. location;
3. problem;
4. why it matters;
5. required action;
6. whether copyediting resolved it.
