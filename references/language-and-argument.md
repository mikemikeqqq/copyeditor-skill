# Language and Argument Editing

## Editing Priority

Apply edits in this order:

1. factual and technical fidelity;
2. claim-evidence boundary;
3. section function;
4. paragraph logic;
5. sentence clarity;
6. concision and rhythm;
7. word choice and surface polish.

## Language Rules

- Prefer direct subject-verb structures where they improve clarity.
- Remove empty framing such as “it is important to note,” “it should be noted,” and vague uses of “landscape,” “realm,” “pivotal,” or “crucial.”
- Replace stacked nominalizations with verbs.
- Repair unclear pronouns and missing comparators.
- Avoid synonym rotation for constructs, variables, theories, and model components.
- Use consistent hyphenation and capitalization for technical terms.
- Avoid em dashes when commas, parentheses, or separate sentences are clearer.
- Preserve field-specific terminology even when a simpler synonym exists.

## Chinese-Influenced English

Common patterns to repair:

- long sentences reproducing Chinese clause order;
- missing articles and prepositions;
- overuse of “can,” “will,” “therefore,” “further,” and “meanwhile”;
- vague subjects such as “this” or “it” without a clear antecedent;
- claims that omit population, context, comparison, or boundary;
- abstract nouns used where an action verb is clearer;
- repeated restatement of the same contribution.

Translate the intended logic, not the original syntax. Identify the claim, evidence, condition, comparison, and implication before revising.

## Paragraph Test

Each paragraph should normally contain:

- one controlling claim;
- the evidence or theoretical basis;
- the logical implication;
- a transition to the next step when needed.

Split paragraphs that perform multiple unrelated functions. Do not split merely to shorten them.
