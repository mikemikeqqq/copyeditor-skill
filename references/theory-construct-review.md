# Theory, Construct, and Terminology Review

## Argument Chain

Trace and test:

1. phenomenon and research problem;
2. unresolved literature problem;
3. theoretical lens;
4. construct definitions;
5. proposed explanatory logic;
6. hypothesis or proposition;
7. empirical test;
8. finding;
9. theoretical contribution;
10. boundary condition and limitation.

Flag any missing bridge, circular reasoning, unsupported inference, or change in construct meaning.

## Construct Dictionary

Record for every focal, control, alternative, and neighboring construct:

- exact canonical label and abbreviation;
- source definition and manuscript-specific definition;
- defining attributes and excluded attributes;
- conceptual domain and referent;
- unit and level of analysis;
- temporal status or time horizon;
- dimensional structure and subdimensions;
- theoretical and empirical role;
- manipulation, measure, proxy, indicator, or coding rule;
- scale source, item labels, anchors, and scoring direction;
- expected relationships;
- neighboring constructs requiring differentiation;
- locations in the abstract, theory, hypotheses, methods, results, discussion, tables, figures, and appendices.

Use the dictionary as an internal audit map. Do not insert it into the manuscript unless requested.

## General Construct-Differentiation Check

For each pair or cluster of potentially overlapping constructs, compare the following dimensions without assuming any predetermined construct pair:

1. **Definition:** What is the construct, and what is outside its definition?
2. **Conceptual domain:** What type of phenomenon does it represent?
3. **Referent:** To whom, what, or which relationship does it apply?
4. **Level of analysis:** At what analytical level is it theorized and measured?
5. **Temporal status:** Is it framed as a stable property, temporary state, event, process, judgment, or consequence?
6. **Dimensionality:** Is it unidimensional, multidimensional, hierarchical, formative, or reflective as claimed?
7. **Nomological position:** What role does it play in the theoretical model, and how is it expected to relate to other variables?
8. **Operationalization:** How is it manipulated, measured, coded, or proxied?
9. **Empirical distinctiveness:** Does the manuscript provide sufficient evidence that it is distinguishable from adjacent constructs?
10. **Terminological stability:** Is the same label used consistently, and are different labels reserved for genuinely different concepts?

A distinction is defensible only when the manuscript explains the conceptual boundary and the operationalization respects that boundary. Different names alone do not establish distinct constructs, and similar wording alone does not establish equivalence.

## General High-Risk Patterns

Flag the following without substituting a manuscript-specific answer:

- the conceptual definition and operationalization capture different domains;
- the construct's referent, level, or time frame changes across sections;
- a dimension, indicator, proxy, manipulation check, or outcome is treated as the construct itself;
- two labels are used for one construct without a stated reason;
- one label is used for multiple meanings;
- neighboring constructs are distinguished rhetorically but not conceptually or empirically;
- item sets are duplicated, mismatched, reverse-keyed incorrectly, or assigned to the wrong label;
- a construct is central to the argument but is not defined, developed, operationalized, or tested;
- a theoretical role changes across hypotheses, analyses, and discussion;
- the manuscript claims discriminant validity or conceptual distinctiveness without adequate evidence;
- construct language becomes broader in the discussion or implications than in the theory and measures.

When the correct distinction requires theory choice, measurement evidence, data access, or reanalysis, add a concise Word comment and report item rather than making an unsupported substantive edit.

## Theory Claims

Edit unsupported certainty to defensible language. Add comments when the theory itself must change. Do not insert a new theory, mechanism, construct, or citation as though it were author-provided.

## Contribution Test

A contribution should specify:

- what prior literature explains;
- what remains unresolved;
- what the manuscript changes in that explanation;
- why the change matters;
- the conditions under which the contribution applies.

Flag contributions that merely restate results, rename an existing idea, combine familiar variables without a distinct prediction, or claim novelty without a defined comparison set.
